# Strategic Alliance Management

## When to Use
- Entering or managing strategic alliances
- Making outsourcing vs. internalization decisions
- Forming cooperative relationships with suppliers, customers, or competitors
- Protecting core skills while collaborating

## Procedure

### Phase 1: Alliance Classification and Selection
1. **Determine Equity Structure**:
   - **Joint Ventures**: Partners take equity positions in new businesses (long-term, high commitment)
   - **Cooperative Agreements**: Nonequity forms (licensing, franchising, turnkey, long-term contracting)

2. **Select Alliance Type by Strategic Objective**:
   - **Collaborative Advertising**: Cooperative TV/promotion (e.g., Amex + Toys 'R' Us)
   - **R&D Partnerships**: Next-gen product development (e.g., biotech alliances)
   - **Lease/Service Agreements**: Financing services provision
   - **Shared Distribution**: Cross-selling in different geographies (e.g., Nissan-Volkswagen)
   - **Technology Transfer**: Joint system development (e.g., IBM-Apple)
   - **Cooperative Bidding**: Collaboration for major contracts (e.g., defense contractors)
   - **Cross-Manufacturing**: Shared production lines (e.g., Ford-Mazda)
   - **Resource Venturing**: Joint extraction ventures
   - **Government-Industry Partnering**: Public-interest projects
   - **Internal Spinoffs**: New companies for specific products
   - **Cross-Licensing**: Territory-specific product sales

3. **Apply Strategic Sourcing Decision**:
   - Outsource where suppliers have distinct comparative advantage (scale, cost structure, performance incentives)
   - Keep in-house where core competence exists
   - Contract out non-core activities

### Phase 2: Alliance Management Principles
4. **Treat Collaboration as Competition**:
   - Enter with clear strategic objectives
   - Understand partners' objectives and their impact on your success
   - Never assume partners aren't seeking to disarm your company

5. **Reject Harmony as Success Metric**:
   - Accept occasional conflict as evidence of mutually beneficial collaboration
   - Recognize alliances rarely remain win-win forever
   - Monitor for partners contentedly surrendering core skills

6. **Enforce Cooperation Limits**:
   - View alliance as constantly evolving bargain beyond legal agreements
   - Inform all employees what skills/technologies are off-limits
   - Monitor partner requests and received information daily

7. **Prioritize Organizational Learning**:
   - View alliance as window on partner's broad capabilities
   - Build skills outside formal agreement scope
   - Systematically diffuse new knowledge throughout organization

## Key Variables
- `alliance_type`: One of 11 specific forms (categorical)
- `equity_structure`: Joint venture vs. cooperative agreement (binary)
- `core_competence`: Activities representing organizational core vs. outsourceable (assessment)
- `partner_objectives`: Strategic goals of alliance partner (concept)
- `off_limits_skills`: Capabilities designated not for sharing (asset)

## Constraints
- Evolving bargain terms require constant vigilance
- Risk of competitive compromise through information leakage
- Boundaries between organizations become blurred
- Power and negotiation aspects dominate strategy formation
- Alliances can close down competition (political dimension)

## Output
Strategic alliance configuration with risk assessment and management protocol.
