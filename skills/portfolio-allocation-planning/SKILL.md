# Portfolio Allocation Planning

Allocate capital strategically across business units or products based on market position and growth dynamics.

## When to Use

- Annual strategic planning and budgeting cycles
- Post-acquisition integration and resource distribution
- Evaluating whether to enter or exit businesses
- Balancing cash generation against growth investment

## Required Data

| Metric | Definition | Source |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Relative Market Share | Your share ÷ Largest competitor's share | Market research, industry reports |
| Market Growth Rate | Annual percentage growth of total market | Industry forecasts, historical trends |
| Cash Flow | Net cash generated (revenues minus costs and investment) | Financial statements |

## The Four Categories

Classify each business unit or product:

### Stars (High Share, High Growth)
- **Characteristics**: Market leaders in growing markets
- **Cash Dynamics**: May self-fund or need supplementation; heavy reinvestment required to maintain position
- **Strategic Imperative**: **DEFEND and GROW**—invest to maintain leadership
- **Future**: Become Cash Cows when growth slows

### Cash Cows (High Share, Low Growth)
- **Characteristics**: Dominant in mature markets
- **Cash Dynamics**: Generate substantial excess cash beyond reinvestment needs
- **Strategic Imperative**: **HARVEST**—take profits, minimize new investment
- **Critical Rule**: Do NOT reinvest excess cash here; returns will decline

### Problem Children / Question Marks (Low Share, High Growth)
- **Characteristics**: Weak position in attractive markets
- **Cash Dynamics**: Require massive cash input; generate little
- **Strategic Imperative**: **DECIDE**—either invest heavily to become Star or divest
- **Risk**: Without investment, become Dogs; with investment, may still fail

### Dogs (Low Share, Low Growth)
- **Characteristics**: Weak position in unattractive markets
- **Cash Dynamics**: Accounting profit may exist, but cash is fully absorbed maintaining share
- **Strategic Imperative**: **DIVEST or LIQUIDATE**—no strategic value
- **Reality Check**: Dogs represent failure to achieve leadership or exit timely

## The Success Sequence

```
Cash Cows → fund → Problem Children → become → Stars → mature into → Cash Cows
         (harvest)      (selective investment)   (defend)         (repeat)
```

## Portfolio Balance Principles

1. **Cash Flow Balance**: Every portfolio needs cash generators (Cows) and cash users (Stars, selected Problem Children)

2. **Pipeline Requirement**: Today's Stars were yesterday's Problem Children; today's Problem Children must become tomorrow's Stars

3. **Eliminate Dogs**: They drain management attention and resources with no strategic return

4. **Limit Problem Children**: Only fund those with credible path to leadership; kill the rest

## Execution Steps

```
Step 1: Plot current portfolio on matrix (Relative Share vs. Growth Rate)
Step 2: Calculate net cash position of each unit
Step 3: Identify funding gap: Cash Cows' excess minus Stars'/Children's needs
Step 4: Prioritize Problem Children for investment (fewest, biggest winners)
Step 5: Set harvest targets for Cows, divestment timeline for Dogs
Step 6: Reallocate freed resources to selected Stars and Children
```

|----------|---------------|--------|-----------------|----------|
| [Name] | Star/Child/Cow/Dog | Defend/Build/Harvest/Divest | $X or Zero | Immediate/Phase/Qtr |

## Critical Warnings

- **Growth is not strategy**: Pouring money into growing markets without path to share leadership wastes capital
- **Share must be relative**: Compare to largest competitor, not market average
- **Experience curve matters**: High share drives cost advantages that fund continued leadership
- **No business grows forever**: Plan for the transition; payoff comes when growth slows, not while it continues
