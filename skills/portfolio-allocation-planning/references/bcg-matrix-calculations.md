# BCG Matrix Technical Details

## Relative Market Share Calculation

Formula: Your market share ÷ Largest competitor's market share

Interpretation:
- 1.0+ = You are market leader (left side of matrix)
- 0.5 = Half the leader's share
- 0.1 = One-tenth the leader's share

Typical threshold for "high share": 1.0 (leader) or sometimes 0.5 (strong #2)

## Growth Rate Thresholds

Historical norm: 10% annual growth as dividing line
- Adjust for industry (tech: 20%+, utilities: 3%)
- Use real growth (inflation-adjusted) for long-term analysis

## Cash Flow Mechanics

### Experience Curve Effect
Costs decline ~20-30% with each doubling of cumulative production. High-share players have produced more, thus have lower costs, thus generate higher margins and cash.

### Growth Investment Requirements
To maintain share in growing market, must grow capacity at market rate. Requires:
- Working capital expansion
- Fixed asset additions
- Marketing and distribution buildout

Formula relationship: Cash needed = f(growth rate, capital intensity)

## Portfolio Patterns

**Ideal Portfolio**: Few Stars, several Cash Cows, selective Problem Children, no Dogs

**Dangerous Portfolio**: Many Problem Children, few Cows (cash crisis imminent); many Dogs (dead weight); no Stars (future Cows missing)

## Limitations and Criticisms

1. **Market definition matters**: Narrow definition exaggerates share; broad definition understates it
2. **Ignores synergies**: May recommend divesting strategically connected units
3. **Static snapshot**: Market positions change; matrix needs regular refresh
4. **Assumes cash is constraint**: May not apply with abundant capital access

## Modern Adaptations

- **McKinsey/GE Matrix**: Adds industry attractiveness and business strength as multi-factor axes
- **Parenting Advantage**: Considers fit with corporate capabilities, not just market position
- **Real Options**: Values Problem Children as options, not just binary invest/divest
