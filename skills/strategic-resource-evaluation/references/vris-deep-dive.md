# VRIS Framework Deep Dive

## Detailed Assessment Questions

### Value Creation Mechanisms
- Cost reduction through process efficiency
- Revenue enhancement through differentiation
- Risk reduction through stability
- Flexibility enabling adaptation

### Rarity Indicators
- Patent protection remaining
- Exclusive contracts or licenses
- Irreplaceable talent with unique combinations
- Geographic advantages (natural resources, location)

### Inimitability Sources Explained

**Path Dependency**: Resources developed over time through unique sequences cannot be instantly replicated. Example: Brand reputation built over decades.

**Causal Ambiguity**: When multiple factors interact and success cannot be attributed to specific elements. Rivals cannot copy what they cannot identify.

**Social Complexity**: Relationships, trust, interpersonal dynamics, organizational culture. These emerge organically and resist systematic replication.

### Substitution Patterns to Monitor
- Technology shifts (digital replacing physical)
- Business model innovation (subscription vs. ownership)
- Platform intermediation (aggregators replacing direct relationships)

## Common Pitfalls

1. **Confusing valuable with strategic**: Many resources create value but not sustained advantage because competitors can match them.

2. **Static analysis**: Resources erode. Rarity today does not guarantee rarity tomorrow.

3. **Ignoring bundles**: Individual resources may fail tests while combinations create inimitable systems.

## Case Examples

**Walmart's Distribution System**: Passed all VRIS tests—created value, was rare in early years, built on complex logistics learning, no substitute for cost advantage in that era.

**Sony's Trinitron**: Initially a core competency (display technology enabling multiple products), became obsolete when flat panels substituted CRT technology.
