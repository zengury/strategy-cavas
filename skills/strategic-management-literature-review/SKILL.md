# Strategic Management Literature Review

Access foundational academic sources spanning the major schools of strategic management thought.

## When to Use This Skill

- Writing doctoral dissertations or theses in strategic management
- Conducting systematic literature reviews on organizational strategy
- Tracing theoretical lineage of strategic concepts
- Identifying seminal works for citation frameworks
- Comparing perspectives across strategic management schools

## Literature Review Workflow

### Step 1: Define Your Theoretical Scope
Identify which school(s) of thought are relevant:
- **Normative** (Design, Planning): How strategy *should* be formulated
- **Descriptive** (Learning, Cognitive): How strategy *actually* emerges
- **Configuration** (Positioning, RBV): What leads to competitive advantage

### Step 2: Locate Seminal Works
Use the key author lists above to identify primary sources. Cross-reference with:
- *Harvard Business Review* for practitioner-oriented foundations
- *Academy of Management Review* for theoretical developments
- *Strategic Management Journal* for empirical research
- *Administrative Science Quarterly* for organizational theory links

### Step 3: Trace Citation Lineage
For any given concept:
1. Identify the original formulation (pre-1980 for classic concepts)
2. Locate key elaborations (1980s-1990s)
3. Find critical responses and extensions (1990s+)

### Step 4: Build Your Reference Framework
Structure citations by:
- Chronological evolution of ideas
- Theoretical school or perspective
- Empirical vs. conceptual contributions
- Practitioner vs. academic orientation

## Citation Quality Indicators

Prioritize sources with these characteristics:
- **High citation count** in subsequent strategic management literature
- **Multiple editions** (indicates enduring relevance)
- **Cross-disciplinary influence** (cited outside management)
- **Foundational concept attribution** (named frameworks: SWOT, BCG matrix, etc.)

## Limitations to Acknowledge

- **Temporal boundary**: 1938-1997 coverage; supplement with post-1997 sources for contemporary developments
- **Language**: Primarily English-language sources
- **Geographic**: Heavy representation of North American scholarship
- **Institutional**: Strong Harvard/Stanford/MIT representation
