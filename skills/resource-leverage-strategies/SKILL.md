# Resource Leverage Strategies

Achieve disproportionate strategic impact by multiplying limited resources through five leverage mechanisms.

## When to Use

- Competing against better-resourced rivals
- Entering new markets with constrained capital
- Pursuing ambitious vision with modest means
- Seeking efficiency in resource deployment
- Building competitive position before competitors respond

## The Five Leverage Strategies

Apply sequentially or in combination based on situation.

### 1. Concentrate Resources
**Principle**: Depth beats breadth—focused application at decisive point

**Actions**:
- Identify strategic focal point (geography, segment, capability)
- Withdraw from peripheral activities
- Mass resources at point of maximum leverage

**Example**: Ted Turner concentrated on global news vision, building CNN with focused resource application rather than diversified media approach.

### 2. Accumulate Resources
**Principle**: Learn faster and borrow smarter

**Actions**:
- Extract maximum learning from every experience
- Document and disseminate lessons across organization
- Secure supplier partnerships to access external innovations
- Acquire critical capabilities through alliances before building internally

**Tactics**:
- Post-project reviews with systematic knowledge capture
- Supplier co-development agreements
- Licensing and technology transfer deals

### 3. Complement Resources
**Principle**: Combine resources to create multiplicative, not additive, value

**Actions**:
- Blend technical, marketing, and operational capabilities
- Balance product development with delivery infrastructure
- Create integrated systems where pieces reinforce each other

**Key Insight**: Resources in combination often exceed sum of parts. A manufacturing capability plus a service network creates solutions competitors cannot match.

### 4. Conserve Resources
**Principle**: Waste nothing; use others' resources when possible

**Actions**:
- Recycle: redeploy resources from declining to growing areas
- Coopt: induce competitors to fight common enemies or enter markets first
- Share: joint ventures, consortia for pre-competitive development
- Delay: let others prove markets, enter after uncertainty resolves

**Tactic Example**: Entice competitor to invest in market education, then enter with superior offering.

### 5. Recover Resources
**Principle**: Minimize cash cycle time

**Actions**:
- Accelerate customer payment terms
- Reduce inventory and work-in-progress
- Speed time-to-market to begin revenue generation sooner
- Design for rapid payback periods

## Execution Framework

```
Step 1: Assess gap between aspirations and resources (stretch magnitude)
Step 2: Inventory current resources by type and deployment
Step 3: Select leverage strategies based on resource gaps:
  - Too scattered → Concentrate
  - Learning poor → Accumulate
  - Siloed capabilities → Complement
  - High burn rate → Conserve
  - Slow returns → Recover
Step 4: Implement with metrics for leverage ratio (output/resource input)
Step 5: Reinvest freed resources into next leverage cycle
```

## Warning Signs of Poor Leverage

- Resources spread thin across many initiatives
- Repeatedly solving same problems (learning not accumulated)
- Capabilities developed in isolation, not integrated
- High working capital requirements relative to competitors
- Slow product development cycles

## Relationship to Strategic Posture

Resource leverage enables "strategic intent"—ambitious long-term goals that appear impossible with current resources. The gap between resources and ambition creates the stretch that drives creative leverage.
