# Competitive Positioning Tactics

Select tactical posture based on relative strength ratios and environmental conditions.

## When to Use

- Entering contested markets or segments
- Responding to competitive threats
- Deciding engagement versus avoidance
- Selecting timing of competitive moves
- Evaluating geographic or positional advantages

## Numerical Strength Rules

Assess your strength relative to competitor(s), then apply:

| Ratio | Tactical Directive | Implementation |
|-------|-------------------|----------------|
| 10:1 | **SURROUND** | Encircle; deny escape; force surrender |
| 5:1 | **ATTACK** | Direct assault with overwhelming force |
| 2:1 | **DIVIDE** | Split enemy force; defeat in detail |
| 1:1 | **ENGAGE selectively** | Fight only with favorable conditions |
| Inferior | **WITHDRAW** | Preserve force; avoid decisive battle |
| Greatly inferior | **ELUDE** | Evade entirely; refuse engagement |

**Critical caveat**: These are starting conditions, not guarantees. Adapt as circumstances change.

## Terrain Classification and Response

| Terrain Type | Definition | Tactical Rule |
|-------------|-----------|---------------|
| **Dispersive** | Home territory; easy dispersion | Do NOT fight; forces scatter to defend homes |
| **Frontier** | Borderlands; contested edges | Do NOT stop; pass through quickly |
| **Focal** | Critical junction; multiple interests | Form alliances; shared interest in control |
| **Deep** | Enemy territory; extended lines | Plunder resources; disrupt supply; do not linger |

## Positional Principles

### Elevated Position
- Occupy high ground; force opponent to attack uphill
- Control rivers and roads; dominate movement
- Level ground favors defense; slopes favor prepared attacker

### First Mover Advantage
**He who occupies the field first and awaits his enemy is at ease.**
**He who comes later and rushes into the fight is weary.**

**Application**:
- Preempt key positions (distribution, talent, technology standards)
- Prepare defenses while opponent must attack
- Force competitor to react on your terms

## The Five Elements Calculation

Before commitment, complete this assessment:

```
1. MEASUREMENT: Map the space (market size, segments, geography)
2. ESTIMATION: Quantify forces (your resources, competitor resources, allies)
3. CALCULATION: Run scenarios (outcomes of different engagements)
4. COMPARISON: Weigh alternatives (which terrain, which timing)
5. VICTORY CHANCE: Determine probability of success
```

**Rule**: Many calculations enable victory; few calculations enable defeat; no calculation guarantees defeat.

## Adaptation Principle

> "As water has no constant form, there are in war no constant conditions."

**Do not repeat tactics that worked before.**

Each situation requires fresh assessment. Victory comes from responding to circumstances in infinite variety, not from formula repetition.

## Execution Workflow

```
Step 1: Calculate strength ratio (your force : competitor force)
Step 2: Classify terrain type for engagement location
Step 3: Determine timing (first mover or responder)
Step 4: Apply Five Elements calculation
Step 5: Select tactical directive from table
Step 6: Execute with commitment to adaptation
```
