# Rhenman Consonance Mechanisms

Achieve organizational-environmental fit through four adaptive mechanisms.

## When to Use

- Designing organization-environment relationship
- Choosing adaptation strategy
- Determining power balance with external systems
- Resolving boundary management questions

## The Four Mechanisms

### 1. Mapping: Reflecting the Environment

**Definition**: Structure organization to mirror environmental characteristics

**When to Apply**:
- Environment is stable and analyzable
- Accurate representation enables effective response
- Environmental segments are distinct and persistent

**Implementation**:
- Create organizational units corresponding to market segments
- Align reporting structures with customer categories
- Match geographic organization to market geography

### 2. Matching: Complementing the Environment

**Definition**: Develop organizational capabilities that fill environmental gaps or needs

**When to Apply**:
- Environment has unmet needs
- Organization can develop distinctive complementary capabilities
- Competitive advantage through unique fit

**Implementation**:
- Identify what environment lacks
- Build capabilities others don't possess
- Create mutual dependency relationships

### 3. Joint Consultation: Cooperative Exploration

**Definition**: Support and cooperate with neighboring systems for mutual environmental understanding

**When to Apply**:
- High interdependence with other organizations
- Shared environmental challenges
- Collective action benefits exceed individual action

**Implementation**:
- Establish industry associations
- Create joint research or forecasting initiatives
- Develop shared standards or infrastructure
- Coordinate responses to regulatory change

### 4. Dominance: Projecting Organizational Influence

**Definition**: Shape environment to match organizational capabilities and preferences

**When to Apply**:
- Organization has substantial power relative to environment
- Environment is malleable
- Proactive shaping beats reactive adaptation

**Implementation**:
- Set industry standards
- Influence regulatory frameworks
- Shape customer preferences through marketing
- Control supplier or distribution channels
- Create barriers to entry

## Selection Framework

| Environmental Condition | Recommended Mechanism |
|------------------------|----------------------|
| Stable, analyzable | Mapping |
| Opportunity for unique fit | Matching |
| High interdependence | Joint Consultation |
| Power advantage, malleable context | Dominance |

## Combining Mechanisms

Organizations may apply different mechanisms to different environmental domains:
- Map stable customer segments
- Match emerging technology needs
- Consult jointly on regulatory issues
- Dominate standard-setting in core technology
