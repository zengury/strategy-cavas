# Henderson's Five Rules for Competitive Maneuvering

This skill outlines the art of business brinkmanship. The goal is to achieve a strategic victory in the minds of competitors, which can then be converted into competitive victory in sales volume, costs, and profits.

## When to Use
Apply these rules when:
- Your firm operates in an oligopolistic or competitive market.
- You seek to persuade competitors to stop short of maximum competitive effort.
- You wish to gain a negotiating advantage without destructive confrontation.

## The Five Rules

1. **Know Competitor's Stakes Precisely**
   Focus exclusively on what the competitor gains or loses in contact with you, not what you gain or lose. This assessment sets the limit on their ability to compromise.

2. **Conceal Your Own Stakes**
   The less the competition knows about your stakes, the less advantage they have. Without a reference point, they cannot judge if you are being unreasonable.

3. **Know Competitor's Character Deeply**
   Understand the competitor's character, attitudes, motives, and habitual behavior. This knowledge is essential for negotiating advantage.

4. **Make Arbitrary Demands**
   Make arbitrary demands to improve your relative position, provided you do not arouse an emotional reaction.

5. **Appear Less Arbitrary Than You Are**
   The less arbitrary you seem, the more arbitrary you can in fact be.

## Execution Notes
- These rules rely on emotional and intuitive factors rather than pure analysis.
- Be aware of the risk of arousing emotional reactions from competitors.
- Competitors may misread signals or stakes.
