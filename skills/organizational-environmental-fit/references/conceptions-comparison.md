# Environmental Conceptions: Detailed Comparison

## Objective Environment (Positivist)

**Core Tenets**:
- Environment has concrete, independent existence
- "Out there" waiting to be discovered
- Strategist's role: find and adapt to reality

**Analytical Methods**:
- Environmental scanning
- Competitive intelligence
- Market research
- PEST analysis, Five Forces

**Limitations**:
- Assumes perfect information possible
- Ignores cognitive and social construction
- Fails in novel, ambiguous situations

## Perceived Environment (Cognitive)

**Core Tenets**:
- Environment is real but imperfectly perceived
- Bounded rationality constrains understanding
- Gap between perception and reality is the problem

**Analytical Methods**:
- Cognitive mapping
- Bias identification and correction
- Scenario planning to expand perception
- Devil's advocacy, dialectical inquiry

**Key Insight**: Strategic errors often stem from perception gaps, not environmental unpredictability

## Enacted Environment (Constructionist)

**Core Tenets**:
- No separate objective environment exists
- Organizations and environments are labels for activity patterns
- World is ambiguous field of experience
- Threats and opportunities are created, not discovered

**Analytical Methods**:
- Sense-making through action
- Narrative and symbolic construction
- Attention shaping and framing
- Relationship creation between events

**The Constellation Metaphor**: Stars exist independently, but "Big Dipper" is created by human pattern-making. Strategists similarly create meaningful environments.

**Implications**:
- Strategy is about creating shared meaning
- Focus on symbolic reality for organizational members
- Action precedes clarity (we don't know what we're doing until we see what we've done)
