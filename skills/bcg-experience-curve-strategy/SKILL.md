# BCG Experience Curve Strategy

Leverage cumulative production volume to drive systematic cost reductions and establish sustainable competitive advantage.

## When to Use

- Entering a new market where first-mover advantage matters
- Competing in manufacturing with significant scale potential
- Pricing aggressively to capture market share early
- Planning long-term production capacity investments
- Defending against lower-cost competitors

## Prerequisites

- Historical cumulative production data
- Unit cost data across production periods
- Ability to scale volume significantly
- Capital for capacity expansion or price subsidies

## Execution Steps

### 1. Calculate Your Experience Curve Rate

Determine your cost reduction percentage when cumulative production doubles:

```
Cost at 2N units = Cost at N units × (1 - Experience Rate)
```

Typical rates: 10-30% cost reduction per doubling of cumulative output.

**Quick estimation:** Compare costs at two points where cumulative production doubled. If cost dropped from $100 to $80, your rate is 20%.

### 2. Project Future Cost Position

Forecast costs at target cumulative volumes:

| Cumulative Units | Cost (20% curve) | Cost (30% curve) |
|-----------------|------------------|------------------|
| 1 (base) | $100.00 | $100.00 |
| 2 | $80.00 | $70.00 |
| 4 | $64.00 | $49.00 |
| 8 | $51.20 | $34.30 |
| 16 | $40.96 | $24.01 |

### 3. Select Strategic Posture

**Aggressive Market Entry (New Markets)**
- Price below current costs to accelerate volume
- Accept short-term losses for long-term cost leadership
- Target 2-3x competitor's growth rate to widen gap

**Defensive Acceleration (Existing Markets)**
- Invest in capacity before demand to stay ahead
- Block competitor volume through preemptive pricing
- Merge/acquire to combine experience bases

### 4. Execute Volume-Driven Cost Reduction

1. **Drive cumulative volume faster than competitors**
   - Price to grow share, not maximize margin
   - Expand distribution to reach untapped segments
   - Standardize products to concentrate learning

2. **Capture learning systematically**
   - Document process improvements
   - Invest in automation as volumes justify it
   - Rotate best practices across facilities

3. **Reinvest cost savings into further volume growth**
   - Lower prices as costs fall (strategic pricing)
   - Expand capacity ahead of demand
   - Enter adjacent markets with transferred experience

## Key Constraints & Warnings

- **Ceteris paribus rarely holds:** Input costs, technology, and competition change
- **Volume commitment required:** Must sustain investment through loss periods
- **Competitor response risk:** Rivals may match price cuts, destroying industry profitability
- **Experience leakage:** Employees and suppliers transfer knowledge to competitors

## Success Indicators

- Unit costs declining faster than industry average
- Market share growing while maintaining or improving margins
- Competitors unable to match price without losses
- Cumulative production 2x+ nearest rival
