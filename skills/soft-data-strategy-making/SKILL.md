# Soft Data Strategy Making

Diagnose and correct over-reliance on quantitative data in strategic decision-making by integrating soft information and maintaining operational immersion.

## When to Use

- Strategic decisions are being made primarily from dashboards, reports, or aggregated metrics
- Strategy feels reactive, delayed, or disconnected from ground-level reality
- Important decisions are delayed waiting for "official" data
- Managers manage by remote control rather than direct involvement
- Strategic surprises occur despite having "all the data"

## Diagnostic: Four Limitations of Hard Data

Evaluate whether your current information system suffers from these defects:

### 1. Limited Scope
**Check:** Does your data capture non-economic, non-quantitative factors?

Hard data excludes critical strategic signals: customer expressions, factory mood, competitor tone, political nuances. These never become "hard facts."

**Action:** Build personal information networks—deliberate webs of contacts who provide unfiltered ground-level observations.

### 2. Excessive Aggregation
**Check:** Is data aggregated to the point of losing essential meaning?

Each aggregation level (department → SBU → sector → corporate) strips context. What matters for strategy often exists in the granular details that aggregation destroys.

**Action:** Demand access to disaggregated source data. Review raw operational inputs alongside summaries. As Richard Neustadt advised leaders: seek "odds and ends of tangible detail" not surveys.

### 3. Timeliness
**Check:** Are you waiting for information to "harden" before acting?

Information hardening requires: events → trends → facts → aggregation → scheduled reports. Strategy making cannot wait while competitors move.

**Action:** Act on timely rumors and observations. Prefer today's soft signal over tomorrow's hard confirmation when windows are closing.

### 4. Unreliability
**Check:** Do your numbers carry false precision?

Quantification introduces distortion. Arbitrary assumptions become "agreed figures" then acquire "sanctity of Holy Writ." No one can disprove them rationally, so they stand unchallenged.

**Action:** Treat statistics as hypotheses to verify, not facts to accept. Cross-check quantitative claims with qualitative reality.

## Core Procedure: Integrate Soft Data

### Step 1: Audit Current Information Diet
List your top 10 information sources for strategic decisions. Classify each:
- Hard: quantitative, aggregated, formal, scheduled
- Soft: qualitative, specific, informal, immediate

If hard sources dominate, proceed to Step 2.

### Step 2: Build Personal Information Systems

**Create contact networks across:**
- Frontline employees (sales, operations, customer service)
- Key customers and suppliers
- Industry peers and lateral relationships
- Government and regulatory contacts

**Establish rhythm:** Weekly unstructured conversations, not formal interviews. Ask: "What are you seeing that doesn't show up in the reports?"

### Step 3: Maintain Immersion

**Weekly immersion practices:**
- Spend time in operational environments (factory floor, customer sites, retail locations)
- Attend customer meetings without predetermined agenda
- Review raw customer complaints, not just aggregated satisfaction scores
- Listen to unfiltered sales call recordings

**Monthly practices:**
- Shadow frontline employees for half-day
- Attend operational review meetings below your usual level
- Review "exceptions" and "anomalies" that were filtered from standard reports

### Step 4: Act on Soft Signals

When you receive soft intelligence (rumor, observation, story):
1. **Assess urgency:** Does waiting for confirmation cost more than acting prematurely?
2. **Triangulate:** Can you quickly verify through another soft source?
3. **Act or monitor:** If competitive window is narrow, act on soft data; if reversible, monitor for confirmation

**Example:** Marketing manager hears a major customer lunched with competitor → act immediately to protect relationship, don't wait for attrition report.

### Step 5: Balance Hard and Soft in Decisions

For each strategic decision, explicitly ask:
- What do the numbers say? (hard data)
- What are the stories saying? (soft data)
- Where do they conflict? (investigate—soft often reveals what hard conceals)
- What is missing from both? (unknown unknowns)

## Warning Signs of Hard Data Overreliance

- Strategy discussions center on variance analysis and metric trends
- Managers request "more data" to delay uncomfortable decisions
- Frontline insights are dismissed as "anecdotal"
- Strategic planning follows the reporting calendar
- Competitors consistently surprise you despite your "intelligence"

## Key Principle

> Hard data may inform the intellect; soft data builds wisdom.

Detached managers with abstracted planners do not make bad strategies—they mostly fail to make strategies at all. Immersion in daily detail, with abstraction of strategic messages from it, produces effective strategy making.
