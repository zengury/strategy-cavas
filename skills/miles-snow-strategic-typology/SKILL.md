# Miles Snow Strategic Typology

Classify and align organizational strategy, structure, and process using four archetypes.

## When to Use

- Diagnosing organizational strategic orientation
- Evaluating strategy-structure fit
- Identifying why strategy execution fails
- Comparing competitive positioning across firms

## The Four Types

### Defender

**Strategic Focus**: Stability and market protection

**Characteristics**:
- Limited product set in narrow market segment
- Sealed domain against competitors
- Competes on price or quality to maintain position

**Structural Requirements**:
- Technological efficiency
- Strict organizational control
- Cost discipline

**Risk**: Market erosion if environment changes

### Prospector

**Strategic Focus**: Innovation and opportunity search

**Characteristics**:
- Actively seeks new products and markets
- First-mover orientation
- May sacrifice short-term profitability for growth

**Structural Requirements**:
- Technological flexibility
- Administrative adaptability
- Tolerance for ambiguity

**Risk**: Overextension, lack of focus

### Analyzer

**Strategic Focus**: Balanced risk management

**Characteristics**:
- Hybrid of Defender and Prospector
- Minimizes risk while maximizing profit opportunity
- Follows successful innovations rather than creating them

**Structural Requirements**:
- Dual capabilities: efficient core plus flexible periphery
- Ability to both defend and explore

**Risk**: Mediocrity in both domains

### Reactor

**Strategic Focus**: None (failure state)

**Characteristics**:
- Inconsistent, unstable responses
- No coherent strategy
- Residual category: inappropriate pursuit of other types

**Implication**: Requires fundamental strategic redirection

## Classification Procedure

1. **Assess market approach**: Stability-seeking, innovation-seeking, balanced, or inconsistent?
2. **Evaluate structure**: Machine bureaucracy, adhocracy, hybrid, or incoherent?
3. **Check alignment**: Does structure support strategy?
4. **Identify type**: Map to Defender, Prospector, Analyzer, or diagnose as Reactor

## Strategic Implications

- **Defender ↔ Prospector**: Fundamental trade-off between efficiency and flexibility
- **Analyzer**: Attempts synthesis but requires genuine dual capabilities
- **Reactor**: Signals strategic failure requiring intervention
