# Mintzberg's Generic Strategic Groups

## When to Use
- Analyzing a firm's strategic position
- Benchmarking against strategic archetypes
- Understanding competitive positioning alternatives
- Classifying organizations by scope and differentiation

## Classification Framework

Classify firms into one of these 11 ideal types:

1. **Niche Players**: Highly differentiated by quality/design, narrow scope (e.g., The Economist)
2. **Pioneers**: Very focused scope, highly innovative, first movers (e.g., early Apple)
3. **Local Producers**: Undifferentiated strategies in geographic niches (e.g., corner gas station)
4. **Dominant Firms**: "Heavy" cost leaders, wide scope, often vertically integrated (e.g., Alcan, GM)
5. **Me-Too Firms**: Similar to dominant firms but not dominant, copycat strategies
6. **Worldwide Replicators**: Heavy marketing, global formula replication (e.g., Coca-Cola, McDonald's)
7. **Professionals**: Established professional services (consulting, engineering, accounting)
8. **Thin Producers**: Huge occasional contracts, extensive design innovation (e.g., Boeing, Airbus)
9. **Rationalizers**: Global production mandates, large geographic segments (e.g., IBM, IKEA)
10. **Crystalline Diversifiers**: Highly diversified, wide scope, differentiated by design via internal development (e.g., 3M)
11. **Conglomerates**: Unrelated diversification by acquisition of dominant firms

## Constraints
- Categories are ideal types; real firms may exhibit hybrid traits
- Classification requires understanding of market scope and differentiation concepts

## Output
Firm classification with strategic group label and archetype characteristics.
