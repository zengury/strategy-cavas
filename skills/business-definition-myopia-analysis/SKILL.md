# Marketing Myopia Critique and Business Definition

## When to Use
- Company defines itself purely by broad market need without considering capabilities
- Strategic planning requires business definition decisions
- Risk of marketing myopia or hyperopia exists
- Balancing market perspective with internal competence

## Procedure
1. **Assess Market Definition**: Evaluate how broadly company defines its business scope (e.g., railroads as transportation vs. railroads as railroads).
2. **Audit Distinctive Competence**: Identify actual organizational capabilities in materials, technology, production processes, and distribution channels.
3. **Test Capability Assumptions**: Verify that proposed market definitions align with existing or developable capabilities. Challenge assumption that capabilities are "limitless" or easily adaptable.
4. **Identify Misalignment Risks**: Check for:
   - **Marketing Hyperopia**: Vision better for distant than near objects (overly broad definitions)
   - **Marketing Macro-pia**: Escalating narrow segments beyond experience or prudence
   - **Operational Detachment**: Cerebral exercises detached from operational reality
5. **Balance Market-Capability**: Ensure strategy builds on usable strengths rather than just market opportunities. Recognize that "words on paper do not transform a company."
6. **Evaluate Environmental Stability**: In unstable/complex environments, collapse distinction between formulation and implementation (entrepreneurial or collective learning approaches).

## Key Variables
- `market_definition`: Business scope definition (string)
- `distinctive_competence`: Organizational capabilities array (materials, technology, production, distribution)
- `environmental_stability`: Predictability of external conditions (categorical)

## Constraints
- Must balance market perspective with internal competence
- Cannot assume unlimited strategic adaptability
- Organizational capabilities are not infinitely flexible

## Output
Risk assessment (high/medium/low) of strategic failure due to capability-market misalignment.
