# Strategic School Contingency Selection

This skill applies contingency theory to match strategic planning approaches with organizational contexts.

## When to Use
- Determining which strategic planning methodology fits your organization
- Analyzing why a current strategic approach is underperforming
- Designing strategic planning processes for new organizations
- Diagnosing strategic misalignment between approach and context
- Planning organizational transformations

## Core Selection Framework

### Step 1: Assess Environmental Conditions
Evaluate the environment along these dimensions:
- **Stability**: Stable vs Dynamic
- **Complexity**: Simple vs Complex
- **Predictability**: Predictable vs Unpredictable
- **Structure**: Delineable vs Novel

### Step 2: Determine Organizational Stage
Identify the organization's lifecycle stage:
- Startup or turnaround
- Strategic programming or assessment
- Reconception or reinforcement
- Maturity or death
- Evolving or unprecedented change

### Step 3: Identify Organizational Form
Classify the organizational structure:
- Machine (centralized, formalized)
- Adhocracy (decentralized, flexible)
- Professional (expertise-driven)
- Missionary (values-driven)
- Entrepreneurial (simple, centralized)

### Step 4: Apply Selection Logic

Use these primary decision rules:

**IF** environment is stable AND simple:
→ Use **Planning School** or **Positioning School**

**IF** environment is complex AND dynamic:
→ Use **Learning School**

**IF** organization is in startup phase:
→ Use **Entrepreneurial School**

**IF** facing political conflict or blockage:
→ Use **Power School**

**IF** environment is incomprehensible:
→ Use **Cognitive School**

**IF** undergoing transformation:
→ Use **Configuration School**

### Step 5: Validate Critical Gaps
For each recommended school, consider the gap between intended and realized outcomes:
- Design School: Intended 'fit' → Realized 'think (as case study)'
- Planning School: Intended 'formalize' → Realized 'program (rather than formulate)'
- Positioning School: Intended 'analyze' → Realized 'calculate (rather than create)'
- Entrepreneurial School: Intended 'envision' → Realized 'centralize (then hope)'
- Learning School: Intended 'learn' → Realized 'play (rather than pursue)'
- Power School: Intended 'grab' → Realized 'hoard (rather than share)'
- Cultural School: Intended 'coalesce' → Realized 'perpetuate (rather than change)'
- Environmental School: Intended 'cope' → Realized 'capitulate (rather than confront)'
- Configuration School: Intended 'integrate, transform' → Realized 'lump, revolutionize'

## Constraints and Warnings

- Best-fit situations are idealized; real-world contexts often require hybrid approaches
- Realized messages represent common critiques that may not apply to all implementations
- Configuration School can integrate multiple schools but may oversimplify complex transitions
- Environmental School assumes organizations are reactive; may not suit proactive strategies
- Cultural School can perpetuate inertia when change is needed

```
Recommended School: {school}
Organization Form: {form}
Stage: {stage}

```
